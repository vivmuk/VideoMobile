HOW IT WORKS

  1. Pick your starting point: image → video, text → video, or video → video.
  2. Describe the shot: subject, action, camera move, light.
  3. Choose model, shape, length and quality. Defaults are already sensible.
  4. Press GENERATE to see the exact Venice price, then press again to confirm.
  5. Leave the screen if you want. The server keeps the job running and the clip lands in HISTORY.

THE FOUR MODES

IMAGE → VIDEO

The default, and the one most people want. You supply one photo and the model animates it. You keep control of the person, product, or place because the first frame is yours.

Transition models live here too: they blend a first and a last frame, so a second slot appears when you pick one.

TEXT → VIDEO

No photo. The model invents everything from your description. Great for ideas, moods, and scenes you cannot photograph.

Write camera direction and lighting, not just the subject.

REFERENCE → VIDEO

Give the model up to five images of the characters, products, or places you want it to keep consistent, then say what happens. The subjects stay recognisable across the whole clip.

Each image is named @image1, @image2 and so on. Tap a tag to drop it into your description, and the studio rewrites it into whatever the model you picked actually reads.

VIDEO → VIDEO

Start from a clip you already have. Restyle it, apply motion from it, or upscale it to a sharper resolution.

Upscale models use an ENHANCE factor instead of a quality setting.

WRITING A BETTER PROMPT

Type a rough idea, then press the wand in the corner of the prompt box. It rewrites your draft into a full shot description using the structure below, tuned to the model, length and shape you have selected. Press it again to undo — your own wording is never lost.

The eight things a video model wants, in one flowing paragraph:

1 · SUBJECT

One concrete subject with the detail that makes it recognisable. "A weathered fisherman", not "a person".

2 · ACTION

One continuous action that fits in the clip. Five seconds is a single beat, not a story.

3 · SETTING

Place, time of day, weather. These do most of the work in setting the look.

4 · CAMERA

Shot size, angle, lens, and one movement: locked off, slow dolly in, tracking, crane up, handheld, orbit.

5 · LIGHT

Source, direction, quality. "Low sun through blinds" beats "good lighting".

6 · ATMOSPHERE

Mood, plus something in the air: haze, dust, rain, steam. It gives the motion something to reveal.

7 · STYLE

Cinematic, documentary handheld, 35mm grain, anamorphic, anime, stop motion.

8 · MOTION DETAIL

Speed, and secondary movement: hair, fabric, water, smoke, reflections.

  • Write prose, not keyword lists. Video models follow sentences far better than comma-separated tags.
  • Say what is there, never what is missing. "No people" often produces people — put it in the negative prompt instead.
  • Stay in one shot. Asking for cuts inside a five-second clip produces mush.
  • With a starting image, describe the motion and what changes — not what is already visible in the frame.
  • For video-to-video, describe the treatment, and let the source clip keep its own movement.
  • Change one thing per retry, so you can tell what actually improved.
